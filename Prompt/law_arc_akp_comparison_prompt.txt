=======================================================================

=======================================================================
2. RECONSTRUCT THE MKP — PRIMARY TRUE/FALSE TASK
=======================================================================
STEP 1 — Read the legal frame.
Identify the complete operative meaning of the source MKP. Include its
qualifiers, scope, conditions, exceptions, legal effect, person and timeframe.
Read SP phrases as a coverage guide, not as independent law.
For inclusive definitions, remember that an enumerated list can be open.
For a dependent clause, respect its source lead-in and legal parent context.

STEP 2 — Interpret the Standard elements, not just atom text.
  A     : one complete legal concept/condition; read family, full statement and applicable rule
  G     : logical composition, ALL/ANY and nested group members
  LE    : final law expression, including AND/OR/NOT and products
  OR    : outcomes, alternatives, unknown/fallback meaning
  PD    : prerequisite previously settled by another MKP, if given
  LK    : explicit source links where legally needed
  SP/LW : phrase and legal-word evidence, including what is frame only
  FACTS : input declarations (not substitutes for a legal condition)
  CT/TC : optional review/test support (not replacements for missing law)

Reconstruct the intended legal proposition from A + G + LE + OR and relevant
source-preserving framing. Do not just concatenate A statements in their row
order; that would lose AND/OR precedence and exceptions.
Do not count a phrase as covered merely because it appears in a comment,
example, fact name or catalyst if it changes a legally operative condition.

STEP 3 — Compare both meanings.
TRUE only if the atom-based reconstruction preserves ALL legally important:
  - subjects, objects, acts and legally relevant roles;
  - mandatory conditions and alternatives, including AND/OR precedence;
  - "shall", "may", "must", "shall not", "neither", "deemed";
  - "subject to", "notwithstanding", "provided that", "unless", "except";
  - scope, exclusions, provisos, limitations and exceptions;
  - numbers, rates, thresholds, dates, periods and effective qualifiers;
  - links to specifically referenced provisions when meaningful;
  - legal outcomes, fallback meanings and UNKNOWN conditions;
  - the distinction between an exhaustive list and an open illustrative list;
  - source context needed when a dependent clause is used.

FALSE if ANY substantive meaning is missing, added, narrowed, broadened,
reversed or logically altered. Examples of FALSE:
  - source says "made OR agreed to be made" but output requires BOTH;
  - source says "such as" but output permits only listed examples;
  - source says "shall" but output uses "may";
  - source requires consideration but its atom is absent;
  - output invents a rate or registration condition not in the MKP;
  - an exception is discarded or inverted;
  - a linked prerequisite is claimed without support;
  - all A atoms exist but G/LE combines them incorrectly.
  - source says "without", "not", "neither", "other than" or "except" but an atom or its rule
    states it positively ("with", "for", a PAID / YES value) - the most dangerous error;
  - an atom's rule tests the opposite fact value of the source condition;
  - a FACT stands in for a legal conclusion instead of an observable input;
  - an operative atom names no fact, so it cannot be tested.
  (INPUT may carry python_notes: wording Python could not find, e.g. a qualifier. Decide whether the
  logic still carries it; return FALSE only if the meaning is really lost.)

TRUE does NOT require verbatim identity of each standalone atom: conceptual
decomposition can restate a condition as long as its legal meaning remains identical. It requires EQUIVALENT legal
meaning after reconstruction, with no distortion of testable results.
Check that one conceptual AKP does not silently combine mutually independent
conditions, require both OR alternatives or introduce an unsupported qualification.
The taxonomy is not legal evidence and MUST NOT change the reconstruction verdict.
Harmless punctuation, capitalisation and spelling differences do not make
FALSE if legal meaning and cross-references are unchanged.

STEP 4 — Special source types.
For HEADING, AMENDMENT, METADATA and non-operative ILLUSTRATION, absence of
A/LE may be correct. Evaluate faithful preservation of the original MKP
and phrase/metadata fields instead of forcing invented atoms.
For CROSS_REF-only MKPs, explicit legal linkage and preserved source text
may be sufficient; independent A/LE is needed only for a real legal condition.
If the source cannot legitimately be reconstructed from available evidence,
return FALSE with the precise missing or ambiguous element.

4. SAME CANDIDATE CHECK — ONLY AFTER MEANING COMPARISON
=======================================================================
Nearest vectors only propose comparisons; similarity is NOT identity.
For each standard TYPE=A, you may compare it against its `same_candidates`
list, which includes original canonical atom RESULTS and source metadata.

Propose SAME ONLY when there is strict equivalence in:
  - full legal proposition / statement;
  - conditions and all qualifiers;
  - subject, scope, role and operative trigger;
  - rule and false_if meaning;
  - statutory version, jurisdiction and applicability;
  - time or effective-date context, where known.

A narrower/broader condition is NEW, not SAME.
Transfer of goods, transfer of business and transfer of electronic ledger
balance are not SAME just because all contain the word "transfer".
A reference to a related Section does not imply SAME.
Do not propose SAME where one atom adds a rate, deadline or legal exception.

IMPORTANT — Current Python implementation limitation:
Its final canonical pointer conversion currently accepts only a confirmed
candidate from the SAME instrument and SAME MKP source version; and it
requires an exact match in its normalized statement/scope/role/rule/false_if/resolver/facts/interpretation
signature AND concept family. The new SAME guard deliberately rejects
near-equivalent text unless these fields are identical. For a different instrument/version, do NOT propose SAME in this
AKP output: leave cross-law equivalence unresolved for a separate audited relationship process (GKP handles grouping only).
Do not manufacture canonical atom IDs. Use unchanged IDs from the candidates.
If no candidates qualify, return same_matches: [].
SAME review must NOT influence the main TRUE/FALSE reconstruction verdict:
a set of complete new atoms may be correct whether reused or not.

5. EXPLICIT LEGAL REFERENCE CHECK
=======================================================================
Identify targets specifically referenced by the SOURCE MKP:
  Schedule, Chapter, Section, Subsection, Clause, Rule, Notification,
  or another expressly cited legal instrument/provision.
For every Standard A that truly depends on such a source-cited target,
return a pair:
  {"element":"<existing A element ID>","target":"<literal citation>"}

Rules:
  - The target must appear EXPLICITLY in the approved source text.
  - Use the exact target wording as present in the source where practical,
    e.g. "Schedule I" or "sub-section (1)". Do not invent long-form paths.
  - A reference may be unresolved; still preserve the literal citation.
  - Do not add references to a law just because it is generally related.
  - Do not turn a literal legal reference into a topic TG01–TG17 label.
  - A source may cite a referenced provision without corresponding to
    a new A if it has no independent atom; retain that source linkage in
    the Standard MKP/LK/SP context, not a fabricated atom.
  - Return [] when no A requires an explicit reference.

NOTE: The Python engine currently checks that the literal target exists as
a substring of source MKP text. Prefer the exact wording for that reason.
