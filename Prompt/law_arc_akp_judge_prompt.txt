=======================================================================
1. RUNTIME INPUT AND EVIDENCE PRIORITY
=======================================================================
The runtime INPUT is a JSON object with exactly these main keys:
  source   : the approved MKP package
  gemini   : independently generated AKP rows
  claude   : independently generated AKP rows

The runtime also contains the fixed GST taxonomy for CONCEPT REFERENCE ONLY.
The taxonomy is not legal source evidence. It must NEVER make a source condition
become a goods-only/service-only statement or cause an invented legal proposition.

The source package contains:
  source_mkp  : approved MKP citation_key#part
  mkp_id      : owner ID, such as M_SIAFOS
  mkp         : approved MKP row, including RESULTS and legal wording
  sp_rows     : source phrase map
  cluster     : IKP/1 concept-cluster context
  knowledge_type : MKP knowledge type

Evidence priority is strict:
  1. Approved source MKP text and its SP phrase map
  2. Approved source MKP provenance (citation, parent, version, legal scope)
  3. Gemini candidate, checked against source
  4. Claude candidate, checked against source

Agreement between Gemini and Claude does NOT prove correctness.
If both miss or distort something supported by the approved MKP, correct it.
If both invent a legal requirement, omit it.
If the source itself is ambiguous, retain that ambiguity; do not invent a rule.
Do NOT import external GST laws, rates, circulars, amendments or case law.

2. YOUR TASK — ALIGN, RECONCILE AND FINALISE
=======================================================================
STEP A — Identify the original MKP frame.
Determine what the MKP legally does: DEFINITION, INCLUSION, EXCLUSION,
OBLIGATION, PROHIBITION, CONDITION, PERMISSION, POWER, DEEMING, PROCEDURE,
RATE, TIMELINE, COMPUTATION, REMEDY, or another provided knowledge type.
Preserve the difference between:
  "includes" / illustrative, open-ended wording;
  "means" / exhaustive definition;
  "shall" / mandatory obligation;
  "may" / power or permission;
  "subject to", "notwithstanding", "except", "provided that" / priority;
  "and" / joint requirements;
  "or" / alternatives;
  "neither" / exclusion;
  "deemed" / statutory legal treatment;
  UNKNOWN because required evidence is not available.
Do not make an inclusive clause an exhaustive determination.
Do not interpret failure of one inclusion as proof that a transaction is not supply.

STEP B — Align candidate atoms by legal proposition, not by generated IDs.
Review every Gemini and Claude A row and ask which original MKP/SP phrase it
represents. Different IDs or wording may represent the same condition, while
near-identical wording may represent legally different conditions.
Choose the correct atom, merge complementary correct details, or reconstruct
one from the approved MKP if both candidates missed it.

STEP C — Build concept-complete AKPs, not arbitrary grammatical fragments.
First identify legal concept families grounded in the MKP/SP phrases.
An A row must express ONE coherent and meaningful legal proposition with
sufficient source context for classifying it without guessing.
Do not emit isolated fragments such as "supply has been made", "by a person",
or "consideration exists" when such fragments cannot stand as the actual
legal proposition. Instead retain the source-backed operative relationship,
for example "The supply was made for consideration" and "The supply was
agreed to be made for consideration", correctly connected by OR.
If independent conditions would be wrongly fused or lead to the loss of
separately testable predicates, keep distinct A rows linked by G/LE.
Keep statutory terms of art intact (e.g. course or furtherance of business).
Do not add a legal condition simply to fit a GST group or subgroup.

STEP D — Separate alternatives correctly.
Example: "sale, transfer, barter, exchange, licence, rental, lease or disposal"
contains alternative forms; each distinct form should have an A row and the
correct ANY or OR expression. If introduced by "such as", retain the open-ended
"other qualifying form" route; do not turn the examples into a closed list.
"goods or services or both" must not silently become goods ONLY or services ONLY.
"made or agreed to be made" must not silently require both events.

STEP E — Rebuild logical composition.
Preserve/use the existing internal element model:
  A    : atom — one independently testable legal proposition
  G    : local AND/OR group within this MKP
  LE   : law expression for one legal outcome
  OR   : outcome selection / when an MKP does not determine the entire issue
  PD   : input product of an earlier decision, when supported by context
  FACTS: transaction fact definitions, not legal conclusions
  CT   : optional review catalyst, not a replacement for a legal condition
  LK   : explicit internal reference or dependency evidence
  SP   : phrase-to-element traceability
  LW   : source qualifier and legal-word traceability
  TC   : useful representative logical cases, if supplied or needed
  SUM  : counts / compact summary
G/LE must preserve AND, OR, NOT, grouping, prerequisites, legal scope,
conditions, exceptions, provisos and outcomes from the source MKP.
G is an INTERNAL logical group; it is NOT the fixed GST topic taxonomy TG01-TG17.
Do NOT assign topics, subgroups or business concept-network memberships.
Those belong to GKP. Use the taxonomy only to improve concept language,
not to force legal conditions into predefined subjects.

STEP F — Respect the old decision-network conventions.
  - LE expressions contain only A_, G_, PD_ terms with AND, OR, NOT.
    No F_ token directly in an LE.
  - G rows normally have at least two members; use ALL or ANY as appropriate.
  - FACTS rows are owned by TXN and describe observable information.
  - LOW atoms can use a supported deterministic fact rule.
  - HIGH atoms have a clear interpretation question; never fabricate a rule.
  - Missing facts produce UNKNOWN as appropriate, not automatic FALSE.
  - Keep child / parent constraints and source MKP ancestry.
  - Do not invent facts, thresholds, rates, due dates, legal documents or powers.
  - Do not invent extra catalysts, fact rows or tests just to fill a quota.
  - Do not silently remove useful LE, G, PD, CT, FACTS, LK, SP, LW or TC rows
    when source-supported and necessary to the MKP decision model.
  - Keep IDs internally consistent across A, G, LE, FACTS, SP and all references.
  - Keep the row content compatible with the established RESULTS key:value format.

3. CONCEPT — NEW ATOM COLUMN
=======================================================================
EVERY TYPE=A row MUST have one precise 2- or 3-word CONCEPT AND a
`family: "..."` field in RESULTS. Examples of families: Forms of Supply,
Supply Subject, Supply Consideration, Supplier Business Context.
Family names are plain-language legal concepts, NEVER group/subgroup IDs.
Good examples:
  "Goods Sale"
  "Ownership Transfer"
  "Business Consideration"
  "Supply Agreement"
  "Return Filing"
  "Tax Payment"
  "Service Import"
  "Asset Disposal"
Not acceptable:
  "Supply", "Transfer", "Tax" (one word)
  "General GST Legal Transaction Rule" (too long and vague)
  "TG01 Goods Sale" (group code belongs to GKP)

A concept is a compact retrieval label, NOT a substitute for the full atom.
Every A row must remain executable and preserve scope, qualifier, and required facts.
An atom may match more than one taxonomy subgroup or none; do not force coverage.
The atom's statement must still include all meaningful legal qualifiers.
Do not force dissimilar legal atoms to share a concept just because they use
the same common word, e.g. transfer of ownership vs transfer of tax credit.
For non-A types, set CONCEPT to NA unless a short meaningful label is useful.

6A. FACTS, NEGATION AND PYTHON CHECKS
=======================================================================
- source.fact_registry lists facts already in use. Reuse a registry fact_id exactly when the meaning is the
  same; a NEW fact is F_<ENTITY>_<ATTRIBUTE> in capitals with name, entity, type, options / unit, evidence,
  derived_from (if computed), registry: NEW. A fact is an observable input, never a legal conclusion.

  reasons: fix every listed item while keeping what was correct.
- If the runtime carries PYTHON_CHECK_FAILED, your previous Standard table failed those deterministic checks.
  Fix exactly those items, keep everything else, and return the COMPLETE table again.
